# OriginalRoot brand identity

## Position

**From field to home, in view.** OriginalRoot is a local food network where a grower, household, and local fulfilment team can follow the same offer, price breakdown, batch, and handoff.

The product makes the route easier to see. It does not promise that technology alone removes every intermediary or proves every claim. Storage, grading, packaging, transport, and local coordination are real work; the platform should name their costs instead of hiding them.

## Name

**OriginalRoot** combines *khet* (field) and *sangam* (meeting or coming together). It speaks to a relationship between growers and customers and leaves room for vegetables, fruit, grains, pulses, and other crops. The 10-character name also fits the 8–12 character range highlighted by an Indian D2C naming dataset; that dataset is directional evidence about domain patterns, not a guarantee of recall or success. See [the naming dataset](https://www.d2cstory.com/blog-posts/how-to-name-an-indian-d2c-brand/).

An initial exact-name web search did not return an obvious agricultural match. This is not a trademark, company-name, domain, or social-handle clearance. Complete those checks and test pronunciation and recall with growers and households before commercial registration or paid promotion.

## Visual system

- Warm paper, field green, and a restrained harvest-clay accent.
- An original vector illustration links a grower, field, route, and home; avoid generic marketplace stock-photo grids.
- Serif display headlines add a human editorial voice; clear sans-serif body text supports dense operational screens.
- Interactive harvest-story steps, role entry points, crop-group filters, and live offer search turn the product story into usable navigation.
- Motion stays brief and respects reduced-motion settings.

## Product voice

- Say what was recorded, who reviewed it, and what remains unverified.
- Describe availability from current approved offers; do not create artificial scarcity.
- Show farmer payout, packing, coordination, route costs, and the customer price separately.
- Treat delivery and payment as local operational commitments, not automatic platform guarantees.
